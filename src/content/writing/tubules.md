Dear sir,

I want to put something to you that I am fairly sure is wrong, and I want to put
it to you _because_ I am fairly sure it is wrong, since I have nobody else who
will tell me why.

Consider a point charge. We say it has a field, and we draw lines radiating
outward, and we agree that the lines are a fiction for the benefit of the eye.
Fine. But the force falls as

$$
F = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}
$$

and the inverse square is not a fiction — it is exactly the behaviour you get if
something conserved is spreading over a sphere whose area goes as $r^2$. That is
not a metaphor. That is a bookkeeping statement about a quantity that does not
get created or destroyed on the way out.

So here is the picture I cannot shake. Suppose the field lines are not a fiction
at all. Suppose they are tubules — actual structure in spacetime, of fixed total
number, fanning out from the charge. Then the inverse square is not a law we
discovered; it is a _consequence of counting_. And the fact that the force
diverges as $r \to 0$ stops being a catastrophe and becomes a statement that the
tubules have not yet had room to separate.

I am aware this is worrying to grasp. I cannot tell you what a tubule is made
of, or what happens when two charges' tubules meet, or why the number should be
fixed. I have a picture and no mathematics, which is precisely the condition I
am always accusing other people of being in.

Tell me where it fails. I would rather be shown than left alone with it.

Yours sincerely, Morayo
