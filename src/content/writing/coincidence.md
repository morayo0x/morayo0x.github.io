Dear sir,

You will forgive me for returning to a matter I had promised to leave alone, but
I have been walking a great deal this week and the walking has not helped in the
way I had hoped.

The question is this: why should electric charge come in whole multiples of
anything at all? We observe it. We have observed it for a century. And yet to
observe a thing and to explain it are not, I think, the same act, and I am no
longer content to be told that it simply is so.

Dirac's argument is the one that has held my attention, because it does not
begin by asking about charge at all.[^1] It begins by supposing a single
magnetic monopole exists somewhere in the universe — anywhere, once, ever — and
then asks what the wavefunction of an electron in its field would have to do.
The answer is that it must remain single-valued, and that this is only possible
if

$$
eg = \frac{n\hbar c}{2}, \qquad n \in \mathbb{Z}
$$

Observe what has happened. We have not measured anything. We have supposed one
object into existence and the quantization of every charge in the universe has
fallen out as a consequence.

> A coincidence which can be derived is no longer a coincidence. But a
> coincidence which can be derived from an object nobody has ever seen — what
> shall we call that?

I have set this beside the tubules I wrote to you about in February, and the
comparison is not flattering to me. That was a picture; this is an argument. I
had thought the two were the same sort of thing. They are not, and I would
rather learn it from Dirac than from an examiner.

Yours sincerely, Morayo

[^1]:
    P. A. M. Dirac, _Quantised Singularities in the Electromagnetic
    Field_, 1931. He needs only one, and it need not be nearby.
