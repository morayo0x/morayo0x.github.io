Dear sir,

Not physics this week. Or not obviously.

I have been listening to a great deal of both, and I have arrived at a
distinction which I suspect says more about me than about either of them.

Mozart returns you to yourself. However far a movement travels, it comes back,
and the coming back is the point — there is a room, and you are in it, and the
furniture has been rearranged in a way you find you had wanted. It is the most
_sociable_ music I know. It assumes a listener with a life.

Beethoven does not assume a listener at all. He assumes a universe, and you
happen to be standing in it. The late quartets in particular seem to be
conducting an argument that would continue perfectly well if you left the room,
and the effect of staying is not comfort but something closer to being permitted
to overhear.

What interests me is that I want both, on different evenings, for different
reasons, and that the reasons map exactly onto a division I have written to you
about before. One of them is for the person at the table. The other is for the
person walking home.

I do not think this is a coincidence. I think I have found the same shape in a
place where I was not looking for it, which is usually a sign that the shape is
mine rather than the world's.

Yours sincerely, Morayo
