Dear sir,

A continuation, though I did not plan one.

I wrote to you in December about there being two of me, and I treated it as a
problem of internal bookkeeping — a mild hypocrisy, mine to sort out. I no
longer think that is what it is, because the arrangement has a second party in
it, and she did not agree to her part.

My mother believes several things about the world that I am confident are not
true. They are not harmful beliefs. They are the ordinary furniture of a life,
and they hold up a great deal that matters to her. When they come up, I say
nothing, and the nothing is not an accident — it is a decision I make quickly
enough that it does not feel like one.

Here is what I cannot resolve. I am willing to spend an entire evening arguing
with you about whether a picture of field lines is permissible, on the grounds
that an unexamined belief is a debt. And then I go home and let a much larger
one stand, and call it kindness.

Either the principle is real, in which case I am exempting the person it would
cost me most to apply it to; or the principle is not real, and what I actually
enjoy is the argument.

I do not like either answer. I suspect the truth is that correcting her would
not be an act of honesty but an act of _display_ — that I would be doing it to
be the sort of person who does it. But I notice how conveniently that conclusion
lets me off, and a conclusion that convenient deserves more suspicion than I
have given it.

Yours sincerely, Morayo
