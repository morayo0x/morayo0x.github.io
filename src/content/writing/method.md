Dear sir,

I want to set down what I think I have learned this year, because I can feel it
about to become a habit, and a habit is much harder to examine than a
conclusion.

I used to believe that criticism was a service one performed on an idea: you
inspect it, you find the flaw, you report the flaw. On that view the value of
the exercise is the flaw, and an idea that survives is one you have wasted an
afternoon on.

I no longer think that is right. Consider what actually happened with the
tubules. You did not find a flaw. You asked what a tubule was made of, and I
could not say, and the not-being-able-to-say was the entire finding. The picture
was not refuted. It was _located_ — shown to be a thing of one kind (a picture)
when I had been carrying it as a thing of another kind (an argument).

So I would put it this way now. Constructive criticism is not a method for
producing verdicts. It is a method of questioning, and its product is not _true_
or _false_ but a clearer account of what kind of claim is on the table and what
would have to be done to promote it.

This has a consequence I do not entirely like. It means that most of what I
believe is not wrong. It is unplaced — held with a confidence appropriate to an
argument, while being a picture. Gödel is somewhere behind this thought and I am
not equipped to bring him forward properly, but I notice that even a formal
system cannot certify its own footing from the inside, and I am considerably
less rigorous than a formal system.

The discipline, then, is not to ask _is this true_ but _what am I holding, and
at what grip_.

Yours sincerely, Morayo
