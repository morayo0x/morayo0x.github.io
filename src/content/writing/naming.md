Dear sir,

We spent ninety minutes last night arguing about what to call a society that
does not exist, has no members beyond the four of us, and has never held a
meeting that was not about its own name.

I want to record that I found this ridiculous at the time and have since changed
my mind.

The proposal was to name it for Animalu. The objection — mine — was that naming
a thing for a distinguished person is a way of borrowing a seriousness you have
not earned, and that we would be better off with something plain until we had
done something. The counter-argument, which I did not have an answer to, was
that nobody will lend you a room for _something plain_.

That is the whole problem, stated in one line. A name is not a description of
what you are. It is a claim about what you intend to be taken as, made to people
who will decide about the room before they ever see the work.

I still think there is something faintly dishonest in it. But I notice that my
objection was aesthetic and their argument was practical, and that in every
previous case where I have held out for the aesthetic objection, nothing got
built.

We are the AAA Mathematical and Physical Society. There is no room yet.

Yours sincerely, Morayo
