Dear sir,

An idea, offered while it is still too young to survive contact with anyone
sensible.

The first-year students arrive able to do mathematics and unable to say what any
of it is _for_. This is not their fault. The syllabus is a list of techniques,
and a list of techniques is not a subject. Nobody tells them that the divergence
theorem is a bookkeeping identity about what leaves a box, or that a Lagrangian
is a bet about which path a thing takes when nobody is watching.

So: a lecture series, run by us, for them. Not remedial. Not a tutorial for the
examination. An hour a week in which somebody stands up and says what a piece of
physics is _about_ before saying how to compute with it.

I have three arguments for it, and I will give the weakest first so that you can
see I am not hiding it.

The weakest is that it would be good for us. Explaining a thing is the only
reliable test of whether you have it. The second is that the department has
rooms which sit empty on Thursday afternoons. The third, and the one I actually
believe, is that there is a particular kind of student who is going to decide in
their first year whether physics is a living subject or a set of hoops, and
nobody is currently in the room when they decide.

I have no funding, no permission, and no name for the thing. I have Thursday.

Yours sincerely, Morayo
