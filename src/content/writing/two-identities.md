Dear sir,

I have been meaning to write this for some weeks, and have put it off for
reasons that will become obvious the moment I begin, which is itself a kind of
argument for beginning.

There are two of me. I do not mean this in the way people say it when they are
being poetic about moods. I mean that the person who sits at my mother's table
on a Sunday and the person who walks home afterwards thinking about the
divergence of a field are not, so far as I can tell, running the same programme.
The first one is patient, and agreeable, and does not correct anybody. The
second one cannot leave a single thing alone.

What troubles me is not the existence of the two. It is that I have never once
caught them in the same room. Each is perfectly coherent on its own terms, and
each seems to regard the other as a costume the family requires.

Is this dishonesty? I have turned it over and I do not think it is, or not
exactly — nobody has been deceived about anything that matters. But I notice I
would be embarrassed if either were shown the other's evening, and embarrassment
is usually the body noticing something before the argument catches up.

Perhaps this is simply what it is to be twenty and have two vocabularies. I
would like to believe that. I am writing it down so that I can be held to it
later, if it turns out to be the sort of thing one ought not to have believed.

Yours sincerely, Morayo
