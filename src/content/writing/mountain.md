Dear sir,

Three men are climbing a mountain.

The first climbs because he was told the summit is worth reaching. He does not
enquire further. When the weather turns he is the first to suffer, because he
has nothing to set against the cold except the instruction.

The second climbs because he wishes to be a man who has climbed it. He suffers
less, being warmed by the version of himself waiting at the top, but he is not
really on the mountain at all — he is already down, telling someone.

The third has no reason he can state. He is simply going up, and when asked why,
finds that the question has the wrong shape, as though one were asked why one
continues to be a person.

It is the third who is the difficulty. He cannot be argued out of it, because
there is no argument holding him there to dismantle. And he cannot be argued
_into_ stopping, for the same reason.

I have spent a great deal of this month trying to work out which of the three I
am, and whether it is permissible that I do not know. The matriculation went
badly. The switch of subject did not come through. I have been asked, more than
once and not unkindly, whether I intend to continue, and I have been giving the
answer of the first man while suspecting the position of the third.

If the summit turns out not to matter, then the climbing was the whole of it,
and death is not a defeat but simply the point at which one stops. I find I can
hold that thought without distress, which either means I have understood
something or that I am too tired to feel it properly.

Yours sincerely, Morayo
